THE HAND BANKS

The rules & bank reference

One message. Two transformations.

  1. Egg transmission: mirror each letter, A to Z, B to Y, and so on.
  2. Golden rule: read the bank's row value. 0 moves up one row; 1 moves down. Stay in the same column and wrap around at either end.
  3. Replace each space with a randomly selected 7, 8 or 9. Attach the bank and window ID.

Reading a transmission

[LHB-20261002-1800] means Left Hand Bank, 2 October 2026, 18:00 to 21:00 Perth time. The decoder first checks that the message bank matches this browser's active bank. Only then does it reverse the row mapping and the mirror.

Windows begin at 00:00, 03:00, 06:00, 09:00, 12:00, 15:00, 18:00 and 21:00 in Perth. Decoding is allowed only within the message's own window, even if its bank returns later.

Preparing your text

Numbers become words: 42 becomes FORTY TWO; 7.5 becomes SEVEN POINT FIVE. Leading zeros and numbers longer than 15 digits are read digit by digit. Punctuation is omitted, apostrophes are removed, and word gaps are standardised. For dates, fractions, phone numbers or special meanings, write the words yourself and check “Prepared message”.

Offline operation

All devices running v2.2 use the same clock-based, pseudorandom bank schedule. A different bank takes over every three hours, at the same instant worldwide. Opening the app, refreshing it or changing device timezone does not change the schedule. A message cannot switch the active bank. No bank state or messages are saved.

Both people must use v2.2 and keep their device clocks correct. Older versions use independent banks and may not match the shared schedule. Bank matching and expiry are local app rules that can be bypassed by editing the code, not secure encryption or destruction. JANUS is a puzzle cipher, not secure encryption. This edition does not decode v1.1 transmissions.
